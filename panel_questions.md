# AGENT4SC 2026 — Panel Discussion Questions

**Panelists:** Ian Foster (ANL/UChicago), Ilkay Altintas (UCSD/SDSC), Sandro Fiore (Univ. Trento), Rosa Filgueira (EPCC, Univ. Edinburgh), Ana Gainaru (LANL), Jose Fortes (Univ. Florida)
---

## Opening / Framing

1. "Agentic AI for science" — is this a genuinely new paradigm, or are we just rebranding workflow automation and calling it AI to chase funding?
2. Most scientific AI agents today are built on top of GPT-4 or similar closed, proprietary models. Is the scientific community sleepwalking into a dependency it will deeply regret?
3. The papers being submitted to workshops like this one — are they solving real scientific problems, or are they solving the problem of publishing papers about agentic AI?

---

## Autonomy & Human Oversight

4. If a human reviews and approves every agent decision, is it actually autonomous? At what point does "human-in-the-loop" become just a liability shield?
5. We say scientists should stay in control — but most scientists don't understand what the agent is doing under the hood. Is "oversight" at that point a fiction?
6. Junior scientists learn by making mistakes and course-correcting. If agents handle most decisions, are we training a generation of scientists who can't function without AI assistance?
7. When an agent autonomously reallocates HPC resources in the middle of the night and causes another group's jobs to fail — who gets fired?

---

## Trust & Reproducibility

8. An agent that made 10,000 micro-decisions to produce a result is, by definition, not reproducible in the way science requires. Should we just accept that, or does it invalidate the result?
9. If two agents trained on different LLMs run the same experiment and get different conclusions, which one is right? What does that mean for scientific truth?
10. Provenance systems capture what happened — but not why the agent thought it was a good idea. Is provenance without reasoning actually useful?

---

## Safety & Accountability

11. We have no legal framework for who is accountable when an AI agent corrupts a years-long dataset. Is the community moving fast enough on this, or will it take a major incident to force action?
12. Security researchers have shown LLMs can be manipulated through their inputs. A scientific agent connected to live instruments is a much more dangerous target. Are we building these systems before we understand how to secure them?
13. Should there be a moratorium on fully autonomous agents in irreversible scientific processes — like drug synthesis or climate intervention — until we have better safety guarantees?

---

## Infrastructure & Power

14. Agentic AI workflows consume dramatically more compute than traditional pipelines. Is the scientific community doing enough to justify that energy cost, or are we burning the planet for marginal gains?
15. The labs and universities that can afford to build and run scientific AI agents are already the best-resourced ones. Is agentic AI going to deepen inequality in global science?
16. If Amazon, Google, and Microsoft control the infrastructure that scientific agents run on, do those companies have de-facto veto power over what science gets done?

---

## Hallucination & Scientific Validity

17. An LLM that hallucinates a plausible-sounding but wrong scientific fact inside an agent pipeline could propagate that error through hundreds of downstream decisions before anyone notices. How is this not an existential risk to data integrity?
18. Current hallucination detection techniques are themselves probabilistic. Are we solving a problem with a tool that has the same fundamental flaw?
19. If we accept that agents will sometimes be wrong, what error rate is acceptable in scientific discovery — and who gets to set that threshold?

---

## Standardization & Ecosystem

20. Every major lab is building its own agent framework. Is fragmentation a natural phase of innovation, or is it a collective action failure the community will pay for in 5 years?
21. FAIR data principles have been around for a decade and adoption is still incomplete. What makes anyone think agents will adopt interoperability standards any faster?
22. Should scientific agent protocols be governed by open standards bodies, or will the first framework to reach critical mass just become the de-facto standard — the way HTTP did?

---

## Long-term Vision

23. If agents can autonomously design experiments, run them, and interpret results — what is the role of the scientist? Are we building tools that augment scientists, or tools that replace them?
24. The most transformative scientific breakthroughs historically came from unexpected, serendipitous observations. Does an agent optimizing toward a predefined objective function systematically eliminate serendipity from science?
25. In 20 years, will we look back at this period as the moment science accelerated beyond anything we imagined — or as the moment we introduced a class of errors into the scientific record that took decades to clean up?
